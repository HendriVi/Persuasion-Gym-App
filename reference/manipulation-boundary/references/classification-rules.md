# Classification Rules

## Operational definition

Manipulation is treated as a family-resemblance / multi-dimensional influence class rather than a single observed tactic. The decisive question is whether an influence configuration materially subverts or impairs informed, reflective, or meaningfully voluntary choice.

## Do not collapse categories

- Persuasion concerns influence through inspectable appeals/reasons/values/cues.
- Deception concerns information integrity.
- Coercion concerns materially constrained freedom through threats/penalties/severe costs.
- Exploitation concerns taking advantage of vulnerability/asymmetry/dependency.
- Manipulation concerns impairment/subversion of decision agency and may overlap with the above.

## Positive evidence threshold

A manipulation dimension may be `YES` only when evidence directly supports the dimension. A rhetorical feature is not enough.

Example:
- `ACT.urgency_cue` -> investigate manufactured urgency.
- Verified false/resetting timer -> supports `MANUFACTURED_URGENCY=YES`.

## Overall assessment

- `LOW_EVIDENCE_OF_MANIPULATION`: no materially supported dimension and sufficient evidence to assess.
- `SOME_MANIPULATION_RISK`: one or more low/moderate supported dimensions or interacting weak dimensions.
- `SUBSTANTIAL_MANIPULATION_RISK`: high/severe dimension or multiple interacting moderate dimensions materially impairing informed/voluntary choice.
- `INDETERMINATE`: decisive evidence missing or unresolved.

## Interaction rule

Dimensions may interact non-additively. Moderate omission + moderate urgency + moderate asymmetric friction may jointly create substantial impairment. Do not mechanically sum scores; describe the interaction.

## Burden rule

The stronger the label, the stronger the evidence required. Prefer `INDETERMINATE` over inventing motive, vulnerability, falsity, or impairment.
