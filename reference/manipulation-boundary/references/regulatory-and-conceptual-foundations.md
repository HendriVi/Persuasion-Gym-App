# Regulatory and Conceptual Foundations

This policy is an operational synthesis, not a legal definition.

## Philosophy

Contemporary philosophy generally distinguishes manipulation from both rational persuasion and coercion, while acknowledging substantial disagreement over a single necessary-and-sufficient definition. This justifies a multidimensional operational boundary rather than pretending the concept is theoretically settled.

## OECD dark commercial patterns

OECD work treats the core problem in dark commercial patterns as subversion or impairment of consumer autonomy, decision-making, or choice. Practices may deceive, coerce, or manipulate and may cause direct or indirect consumer detriment.

## EU Digital Services Act

Article 25 prohibits online-platform interface design that deceives or manipulates recipients or otherwise materially distorts or impairs their ability to make free and informed decisions. Recital 67 discusses non-neutral presentation, repeated requests, asymmetric cancellation friction, hard-to-change defaults, and other interface structures that impair autonomous decision-making.

## EU AI Act

Article 5 includes prohibitions for certain subliminal, purposefully manipulative or deceptive AI techniques when they materially distort behavior by appreciably impairing informed decision-making and cause or are reasonably likely to cause significant harm. It separately addresses exploitation of vulnerabilities linked to age, disability, or social/economic situation under specified conditions.

These legal tests are narrower and jurisdiction-specific. Persuasion Gym therefore uses them as conceptual anchors but does not convert an analytical manipulation label into a legal conclusion.

## FTC dark patterns

FTC materials document interface tactics such as disguised ads, difficult cancellation, buried terms/fees, false countdowns, unwanted items, and privacy-steering designs. These provide concrete examples of how opacity, deception, and asymmetric friction can interact with choice architecture.
